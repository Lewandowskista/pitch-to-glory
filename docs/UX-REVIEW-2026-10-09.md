# Pitch to Glory: end-user design and UX review

Reviewed on 9 October 2026 in the local running app at `http://127.0.0.1:5173`.

**Implementation follow-up:** Findings 1, 4 and 9 are addressed by [Phase 1](UX-PHASE-1-2026-10-09.md). The observations below retain the original review baseline; the remaining findings are scheduled in the [implementation plan](superpowers/plans/2026-10-09-ux-improvements.md).

## Overall impression

The game has a coherent, recognisable visual identity. Forest green, warm cream, gold, condensed sports typography and authored vector artwork belong together. The landing screen is convincing, and the interface feels considered rather than improvised.

The biggest weakness is how much administration surrounds the football. Many screens use the same large panels, meters and explanations. They communicate the systems, but give the player fewer reasons to feel attached to their footballer, teammates and club. Improving hierarchy, pacing and storytelling would do more than replacing the visual style.

## Scope and evidence

- Created Alex Morgan, a 17-year-old English striker with the Finisher archetype, from a fresh browser session; generated a world and signed for Gateshead Town.
- Followed the seven-step first-week tour and the first-match tour through its key-moment instruction.
- Played a full competitive match using decisions and the next-key-moment control, including the half-time response, and inspected the recorded report.
- Inspected the hub, profile, skills, training, club life, transfers, media, rival, wardrobe, calendar, Chronicle, world, save slots and settings.
- Inspected desktop at 1440 × 900 and 1440 × 1000, tablet at 768 × 1024, and phone at 390 × 844, with both light and dark themes.
- Verified that training edits disappear when switching tabs, skill selection jumps to the bottom of the phone page, browser Back closes a save deletion confirmation, and `?` / Escape open and close the shortcut guide.

This is an expert walkthrough of an early career, not a player study or a complete accessibility, performance, cross-browser or late-career certification. Audio quality and real-device touch behaviour were not assessed. Browser console inspection reported no warnings or errors during the walkthrough. No application code was changed.

## Keep these strengths

1. **The visual identity.** The landing illustration, green/gold contrast and bold headings give the game a recognisable football personality. Dark mode feels deliberate and belongs to the same identity.
2. **The primary action.** The next fixture and its gold button are easy to find. Explaining what Continue will simulate helps players understand the consequences of advancing time.
3. **Decision transparency.** Success chances, stakes, relevant attributes and optional factor breakdowns build trust. Keep the depth, while reducing the amount shown before it is requested.
4. **Career creation previews.** Appearance thumbnails, the ageing preview, player summary and editable signing review make creation tangible.
5. **Navigation and comfort features.** Stable section tabs, labelled bottom navigation, unread badges, text scaling, reduced motion and simulation-only mode are useful foundations. Save deletion had a confirmation, and Back dismissed it during the walkthrough.

## Prioritised findings

### 1. High: training changes disappear without warning

**Observed:** Changed Session 1 from Normal to High. The page displayed “Unsaved changes.” Opened Profile, returned to Training, and Session 1 was back to Normal. No confirmation appeared. Other parts of the app advertise automatic saving, so this difference is easy to miss.

**Player impact:** A player can believe they have changed their development plan while continuing with the old one.

**Recommendation:** Preserve the draft across navigation, or warn before discarding it. Keep the save action visible near the controls on phones and distinguish an unsaved training draft from the saved career status.

**Location:** `src/screens/career/CareerTraining.tsx:143`, `:164`, `:361`.

### 2. High: key moments require too much scrolling to compare choices

**Observed:** At 1440 × 900, a four-choice moment extended below the viewport even after the game brought the live layout into view; statistics and commentary followed underneath the choices. At 390 × 844, the game correctly scrolled to a compact situation preview, but only the first two choices and part of the third were visible in the observed shooting moment. The fourth required more scrolling.

**Player impact:** The player must remember earlier choices while reading later ones. A decisive football moment becomes a long form to scan.

**Recommendation:** Show a compact comparison of all actions first: name, chance and a brief consequence. Expand the attributes and full factors on request. On desktop, keep score, pitch, choices and a compact live-stat summary together. On phones, use a compact decision layout with the situation still visible.

**Locations:** `src/screens/match/DecisionPanel.tsx:114`, `src/styles/match.css:318`, `:430`.

**Evidence:** [Desktop moment](../artifacts/ux-review/12-keymoment-desktop-900.png), [phone moment](../artifacts/ux-review/11-next-moment-mobile.png).

### 3. Medium: skill browsing breaks spatial continuity on phones

**Observed:** The phone skill page was approximately 4,300 pixels tall. Selecting Clinical Finisher near the beginning scrolled to its detail below all seven branches, with a recorded scroll position of 3,480 pixels. A “Back to the tree” control returns the player, but comparing skills means repeatedly travelling between distant parts of the page.

**Player impact:** Exploring a skill feels heavier than it needs to, and the player loses the surrounding progression context.

**Recommendation:** Open details beside the selected skill, in a dialog/sheet, or directly beneath it. Add branch selection and make prerequisites visibly connected. Keep the current keyboard support.

**Locations:** `src/screens/career/CareerSkills.tsx:109`, `:157`, `:179`.

**Evidence:** [Phone skill detail](../artifacts/ux-review/19-skills-detail-mobile.png), [desktop skill layout](../artifacts/ux-review/skills-desktop.png).

### 4. Medium: the tablet sidebar has horizontal overflow

**Observed:** At 768 × 1024, the main sidebar navigation had a client width of 171 pixels and a scroll width of 186 pixels. A horizontal scrollbar appeared; the Overview unread badge sat against the clipped edge.

**Player impact:** Navigation feels cramped and unfinished at a common tablet width, despite the page itself avoiding horizontal overflow.

**Recommendation:** Let navigation labels shrink or wrap while retaining room for badges, or use the compact navigation earlier. Verify both unread counts and enlarged text at the breakpoint.

**Locations:** `src/styles/responsive.css:74`, `src/styles/shell.css:36`, `:84`.

**Evidence:** [Tablet hub](../artifacts/ux-review/21-hub-tablet.png).

### 5. Medium: the mobile hub prioritises a large fixture card over player status

**Observed:** On a 390 × 844 phone viewport, the heading, tabs and next-fixture card occupied almost the whole first screen. The player's portrait, progression, recent performance and condition were farther down a hub approximately 4,300 pixels tall.

**Player impact:** The next action is clear, but the screen does less to answer “How is my footballer doing?” before the player continues.

**Recommendation:** Place a compact player summary with fitness, fatigue and level near the next action. Shorten the repeated advance-time explanation after the player understands it. Give urgent tasks precedence over empty summaries.

**Location:** `src/screens/career/CareerHub.tsx:138`.

**Evidence:** [Phone hub in dark mode](../artifacts/ux-review/16-hub-dark-mobile.png).

### 6. Medium: career onboarding has too many introductory steps

**Observed:** Six creation steps are followed by a seven-step hub tour and a six-step match tour. The world step exposes a seed and a separate Build World action. Reaching play then involves the hub, fixture selection, briefing and kickoff.

**Player impact:** A new player has to absorb a considerable amount of interface before discovering whether they enjoy the core game.

**Recommendation:** Offer a default world immediately, put seed controls behind an advanced option, and teach hub features when first used. Retain the interactive first-match guidance, which is more closely tied to the player's goal. Reduce repeated fixture confirmation when the player already chose the next match.

**Locations:** `src/screens/career/CareerNew.tsx:23`, `:723`, `src/ui/Tutorial.tsx:20`.

### 7. Medium: the story screens feel too much like numerical reports

**Observed:** Club life presents morale, chemistry, clique influence, culture fit and selection as numerous bars and modifiers. The early media screen had an empty questions panel, a coverage meter and an empty feed. The report's generic praise and headline communicate success, but rarely name the specific contribution.

**Player impact:** The game explains its mechanics better than it makes teammates, supporters and career events memorable.

**Recommendation:** Lead with a specific human interpretation: who trusts the player, why, what changed this week, and one useful next action. Put exact modifiers behind disclosure. Use existing career events for factual welcome/debut coverage and refer to the player's actual assist, rating or performance in report reactions. Give Chronicle, media and dressing-room screens distinct compositions within the same palette.

**Locations:** `src/screens/career/CareerClub.tsx:157`, `src/screens/career/CareerMedia.tsx`, `src/screens/match/Report.tsx`.

**Evidence:** [Club life](../artifacts/ux-review/club-desktop.png), [early media](../artifacts/ux-review/media-desktop.png), [match report](../artifacts/ux-review/13-postmatch-desktop.png).

### 8. Medium: the starting-club choice gives facts without enough guidance

**Observed:** Trial cards show league, stadium capacity, reputation, playing style and manager name. Reputation is an unqualified number. They offer little immediate help comparing the clubs for the selected player archetype.

**Player impact:** The first meaningful career decision can feel arbitrary.

**Recommendation:** Add plain-language, data-backed comparisons of development environment, style fit and competition for the player's position. Explain the reputation scale. Preview the actual contract terms before signing. Avoid promising playing time the simulation cannot guarantee.

**Location:** `src/screens/career/CareerNew.tsx:804`.

**Evidence:** [Trial offers](../artifacts/ux-review/03-trials-desktop.png).

### 9. Low: terminology exposes the asset-collection foundation

**Observed:** Navigation uses “Asset gallery” and “Save collections.” A slot visibly marked Career still offers “Load world,” “Replace with world” and “Delete collection.” These terms are technically meaningful but less natural in a football career game.

**Player impact:** The interface feels like a tool for managing generated content in places where the player expects to manage their career.

**Recommendation:** Use player-facing labels such as “Gallery,” “Saved games,” “Continue career” and “Delete save,” adapting slot actions to their contents. Keep development/gallery storage support behind those simpler labels.

**Locations:** `src/i18n/en.ts:9`, `:46`, `src/screens/SavesContent.tsx`.

**Evidence:** [Save screen](../artifacts/ux-review/saves-desktop.png).

## Suggested order of work

1. Prevent lost training drafts and fix tablet navigation overflow.
2. Make key moments fit a compact, comparable decision layout.
3. Keep mobile skill details close to the selected skill and bring player status higher in the hub.
4. Reduce onboarding friction and clarify trial offers.
5. Strengthen human storytelling, screen variety and player-facing terminology.

The target is the same recognisable game with less reading and scrolling between meaningful decisions, and more feedback that makes the player care about their career.
