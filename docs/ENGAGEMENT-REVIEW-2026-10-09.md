# Engagement review — 9 October 2026

**Scope.** After the [game design review](GAME-DESIGN-REVIEW.md) and its balance passes A–E, the systems hold: careers rise from tier 4 to tier 1 by 20, decisions are honest and the world moves. This review asks the next question: is it fun to play, season after season? It looked at content variety, pacing, drama and long-term goals.

**Method.** Two code audits (the match experience; the weekly loop and narrative), with their counts checked in code; two careers played to retirement with `npm run profile` (a striker and a centre-back, seeds `fun-st`, `fun-cb`); the hub, match and report screens.

## Findings

1. **Match moments are repetitive and blind to the game.** Ten situation types with one fixed sentence each; a centre-back met "An attacker is running at your back line" in ~58% of moments, 4–5 times a match. Moment minutes were fixed evenly from 6′ to 84′, so nothing was ever decided late; no penalties, free kicks or shootouts to play. Finals and derbies looked like league games; international matches are simulated. Simulating a match does as well as playing it.
2. **The world changes numbers, not stories.** ~56 inbox kinds with one template each, 9 press topics × 3 answers, 21 social templates: by season 3 a player has seen nearly all of it. The only multi-week arc is the manager's promise. Six named teammates never speak; the dressing room produces no events. Reactions were "pleased" or "steady" by rating alone (a 0–4 defeat could please the manager). No preseason, deadline day or season review; promotion passes silently.
3. **The career has no shape after the rise.** From ~25 to 34 both profiled careers stayed at one club, offers fell to 1–3 a season, fan affection sat at 96–99, and money piled up unspent (550–650k). The striker finished #1 of 60,851 in the Hall of Fame under a policy that just takes the best offer; the centre-back, top 2% of the world for a decade, won no season award and ranked 806th.
